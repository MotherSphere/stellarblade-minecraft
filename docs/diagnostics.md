# Diagnostic commands

The gameplay path is [`/stellarview play`](../README.md#start-a-session). The commands below isolate problems; they are not required for each session. F10 belongs to the existing Stellar Blade console. Shift may also trigger native dodge; prefer console equivalents when keeping EVE still.

## Stellar Blade

| Key | Console command | Action |
|---|---|---|
| F5 | `sbmc mirror` | Import the active Minecraft collision patch |
| F8 | `sbmc enter` | Enter the created lab once |
| F6 | `sbmc status` | Log context, position and timing counters |
| F11 | `sbmc restore` | Revoke input, stop captures, return EVE and clean up owned geometry |
| F4 | `sbmc framehd` | One 1280 × 720 paired still: BMP color, PNG mask, HDR depth |
| F2 | `sbmc depth` | Independent depth probe; not paired with another capture |
| F1 | `sbmc live` / `sbmc liveoff` | Legacy 320 × 180 animated diagnostic; capped at 60 seconds or 300 frames |
| F7 | `sbmc create` | Standalone collision fixture: floor, four walls, step and obstacle |
| F9 | `sbmc toggle` | Toggle the standalone fixture's obstacle |
| Shift+F7 | `sbmc texture` | Toggle a non-colliding stone-texture probe |
| Shift+F8 | `sbmc visuals` | Toggle native Minecraft terrain visuals inside Stellar Blade |

`sbmc posetest` records EVE's skeletal mesh components for up to 15 seconds or 900 frames: bone transforms relative to their parents (`SnapshotPose`, with a per-bone fallback), face morph weights and component world transforms. It writes private `eve-pose-probe.txt` (components, bones, method, timing) and `eve-pose-probe.bin` (per frame: frame number, seconds, then each component's world transform followed by its bones and weights, 10 floats per transform). It changes nothing in the scene; Minecraft can replay it with [`/stellaravatar replay`](#avatar-view-experimental).

`sbmc frame` retains the 320 × 180 PNG still; `sbmc framehdpng` retains HD PNG color. `sbmc capture` is an independent color probe. `sbmc capturecleanup` retries failed capture cleanup.

`sbmc exportbmp` compares synchronous color exports. `sbmc exportasync` and `sbmc frameasync` share one asynchronous submission attempt per Lua lifetime and omit completion callbacks. The latter requires Minecraft's file-close watcher already running, then `/stellarpreview load` or `/stellarview load`. Pending generation files are retained until cleanup with both games closed. `sbmc exporttest` is disabled because its callback crashed the pinned loader.

Read `SB/Binaries/Win64/ue4ss/UE4SS.log`, prefix `[StellarCollisionProbe]`, and Minecraft's `logs/latest.log`. Successful object creation or texture assignment is not proof of valid pixels or collision.

## Minecraft views

- `/stellarpreview load` shows a still on a checkerboard; `/stellarpreview off` closes it.
- `/stellarview load` composes a still against world depth. It requires the matching entered terrain session and revision.
- `/stellarview live` consumes the legacy F1 producer. `/stellarview livehd` requests the continuous raw producer without input forwarding.
- `/stellarview status` reports image delivery separately from Minecraft FPS; see [performance measurements](#performance-measurements). `/stellarview off` restores the normal camera and stops forwarded controls.

Still captures expire 30 seconds after capture. Legacy live startup times out after five seconds; two seconds without a fresh generation stops an established view. A repeated frame does not renew freshness. Revision changes hide incompatible frames. Unsupported effects, session/world changes or invalid data stop composition.

A high Minecraft FPS counter with slow EVE motion indicates a different capture/transfer cadence; compare accepted frames over the same interval and framebuffer size. A small checkerboard preview does not show the full capture resolution. Hair noise can originate in native capture, not only the Minecraft compositor.

## Avatar view (experimental)

These commands draw EVE with her own meshes in Minecraft. Minecraft keeps its own camera, frame rate, lighting (lightmap at EVE's position, entity-style directional shading) and fog. Her materials add normal maps, ambient occlusion, roughness-based highlights, reflections of Minecraft's sky (in proportion to the sky light where she stands), metals, emissive parts and light scattered under the skin on top of that lighting; hair cards get strand highlights, darker roots and soft strand edges blended over what lies behind them, and translucent overlays (eye occlusion, catchlights, the wet tear line) draw last.

- `/stellaravatar replay` loops the latest `sbmc posetest` recording from the bridge directory two blocks ahead of the player, interpolating between recorded frames. Stellar Blade does not need to run.
- `/stellaravatar live` streams EVE's current pose from Stellar Blade (Lua revision 47 and the matching adapter) once per native frame. After `/stellar start`, F5 and F8 she stands where the bridge places EVE and replaces the player's body in third person; Minecraft hides her in first person, where the camera is inside her head. Without an entered link she stands two blocks ahead of where the command ran. A changed composition (outfit, weapon, hair) briefly reloads meshes and textures.
- `/stellaravatar play`, after `/stellar start`, F5 and F8 and without `/stellarview`, drives EVE from Minecraft without any capture. Minecraft owns the camera (third person by default) and its mouse look; Minecraft's movement and action bindings are forwarded as with `/stellarview play`, gated by a fresh streamed pose instead of a captured image. While input is forwarded, the adapter turns Stellar Blade's camera toward Minecraft's at each native frame: yaw through the game's own look input, as a mouse would, pitch by setting the game's camera dolly from the camera's own pitch (through look input instead while the game's camera follows the controller, eases or lags its rotation, uses another dolly spline, aims, fishes or runs its slope camera). Each pose reports the controller's rotation; the left stick is rotated by any remaining difference so EVE moves where Minecraft looks. If the camera does not follow (a yaw gap above 30 degrees for a second), the right stick steers it instead (full deflection at 45 degrees of yaw error, never reversing during fast spins). `/stellaravatar status` reports the share of poses turned by look input and the mean and maximum yaw gap, then the share of poses whose dolly the adapter set and their mean and maximum pitch gap (Minecraft's pitch against the game camera's, read one native frame earlier). The camera follows EVE's displayed position.
- `/stellaravatar status` reports the stream: component-table generation, poses received per second, incomplete poses, the age of the last pose and the adapter's read time (last, maximum and mean microseconds per native frame).
- `/stellaravatar off` removes her and releases the stream.
- `/stellaravatar shading entity` draws her through Minecraft's entity programs, as under a shader pack, to compare without Iris; `/stellaravatar shading auto` returns to her own shaders unless a pack is in use. `/stellaravatar status` ends with which one draws her.

Each command reports drawn material sections, meshes missing from the pack and, for a replay, loading time. With `/stellaravatar live` alone, drive EVE in Stellar Blade; `/stellarview play` also works but adds its captured image and image-paced camera. Poses are displayed one sample interval behind the newest, interpolated on Stellar Blade's publication times, so motion stays smooth above Stellar Blade's frame rate; `/stellaravatar status` shows that interval.

The renderer reads a private avatar pack from `~/.local/share/stellar-blade-minecraft/avatar` (override with `-Dstellarbridge.avatar=<dir>`). The pack is built from the user's own game installation and is never part of this repository:

- `<mesh>.sbav` for each mesh named in the recording's `COMPONENT` lines. Components without a mesh in the pack are skipped and listed.
- `<material slot>.png`: one baked RGBA base texture per drawn section; alpha below 0.5 is cut out, and its mipmaps keep the share of texels that pass, so thin strands do not thin out at a distance. Hair slots then blend what the cut-out dropped over the opaque image without writing depth, so strand edges are smooth; overlapping soft edges are not sorted. Sections without a PNG are not drawn.
- Optional maps beside it, each falling back to a neutral default: `<slot>.normal.png` (tangent-space normal in Unreal's convention, +X along +U and +Y along +V; the shader builds the frame from screen-space derivatives, so meshes need no tangents), `<slot>.surface.png` (occlusion, roughness, metallic and specular in R, G, B, A; specular 0.5 is 4 % reflectance), `<slot>.emissive.png` (RGB added after lighting), `<slot>.scatter.png` (subsurface color times mask: light wraps past the terminator along the smooth normal) and `<slot>.hair.png` (root-to-tip gradient, strand id and depth; selects strand shading along +V).
- `<slot>.overlay.png` instead of `<slot>.png` for a translucent slot: premultiplied RGB with its opacity in alpha (alpha 0 adds light), drawn after every opaque section without writing depth; without its own surface map it gets no highlight. `<slot>.glow.png` is such an overlay drawn only while the slot's streamed `Opacity` is above zero, its emissive map tinted by `Emissive_Color` × `Emissive_Intensity` plus the `_2` pair.
- `<mesh>.trail.txt` beside a weapon mesh: `SBAVTRAIL 1`, `BEGIN bone x y z` and `END bone x y z` (the trail sockets in Unreal units in their bones' space), `HELD mesh handBone centimeters chestBone`; with `<mesh>.trail.png` (gradient in red, streak noise in green).
- `fx.<texture>.png`: Stellar Blade's own effect textures (sparks, flares, rings, lightning arcs, wind streaks), glow in red. Without them, Minecraft particles stand in for the game's effects.

`SBAVMESH` version 2 is little-endian, in Unreal space (centimeters, Z up): magic `SBAVMESH`, `u32` version; `u32` bone count (at most 256), then per bone a `u16`-length UTF-8 name, `i32` parent (earlier bone or -1) and the reference local transform (10 floats: quaternion xyzw, translation, scale); `u32` vertex count, then 56-byte vertices (position, normal, UV, four `u16` bone indices, four float weights); `u32` index count and `u32` triangle indices; `u32` section count, then per section a material slot name, first index and triangle count; `u32` morph count, then per morph a name, `u32` delta count and per delta a `u32` vertex with position and normal deltas (6 floats). Morphs follow the mesh's Unreal `MorphTargets` order and are matched to recorded weights by name. Recorded bone names must equal the mesh's bones in order; leader-pose followers (face, ponytail) map bones by name to their leader.

The live stream carries the face's morph weights (expressions, blinks); `sbmc posetest` recordings do not. It also carries glow parameters the game animates on dynamic material instances (`Opacity`, `Emissive_Intensity`, `Emissive_Color` and their `_2` pairs): a `<slot>.glow.png` slot such as the sword's edge appears only while its `Opacity` is raised. `/stellaravatar status` lists how many parameters are streamed and the highest glow opacity seen. Most of Stellar Blade's combat effects are not material parameters but particle systems its attack shows spawn (anim trails between weapon sockets, Niagara effects such as the sword flame buff). The sword's trail is a short ribbon (0.12 s by default) between the weapon's trail sockets with the game's trail gradient. While effects are streamed it follows the game's own Cascade trails (`P_A_AnimTrail_*`): it draws only while one plays, with that trail's emitter colors and lifetime (white fading to blue for light slashes, a longer white for strong ones, bluish thrusts). Without effect data it falls back to the swing: while the blade is in EVE's right hand and turns fast relative to her chest. The game's other effects on EVE are particle components the world pools and attaches to her skeletal components while they play. Lua publishes how to read them (reflected offsets and the effect assets: the sword's main ones once loaded, then any seen active) and the adapter reports up to eight active ones each frame with their world transform. Minecraft redraws them as additive sprites with the game's textures, one recipe of emitters per asset approximating its Niagara emitters: sparks streaking from the blade (`NS_A_ParTrail_*`), the charged heavy attack's red horizontal flare, lens flare, sparks and crystal shards (`*HoldBurst_Signal*`), a red ring with orange sparks (`*HoldBurst_SwordFX*`), blue lightning arcs along the blade (`*Electric*`), flares for draws and kicks, wind streaks around EVE for spins, mesh trails and charged slashes, a small flare otherwise. Without `fx.*` textures it spawns Minecraft particles there instead. `/stellaravatar status` counts known effects and poses with active ones. Lua also logs every change of EVE's active effects to `UE4SS.log` as `EVE effects: asset[class]@parent:socket ...`. `./gradlew avatarRenderCheck -PavatarCheck="<pack> <recording dir> <out.png> <seconds,...> [distance,height,degrees [glow | fx=asset+asset]]"` in `minecraft/` renders a pack and recording offscreen with the same renderer and prints per-mesh extents; `fx=` plays those effect assets at the blade for 0.2 s before each frame. It needs a GPU and is not part of `tools/check.py`.

Lua logs each combat event it acts on as `Combat: kind seq [from yaw deg]; show ...; effects ...` (a failed Show or effect says why); a perfect dodge logs EVE's animation state before the Show (`before: custom alpha A; move block N; move type T; input F/R`), 0.2 s later `Combat: perfect_dodge seq: Stellar Blade plays it; +0.2s: ...` or `... show not seen; time at 0.30; effects ...`, and `Combat: perfect_dodge seq +0.6s: ...`, then `Combat: time restored` after a fallback; a perfect parry logs both Shows (`show P_Eve_Sword_Normal_Guard1_JustParry ...; show Result_JustParry_FX ...`, with `effects NS_JustParry_02` only when the latter failed), a guard `show Result_Guard_Back... toward the side (deg), hit-level check lifted`, and each of them, like a parry, `Combat: kind seq +0.6s: custom alpha A; ...` (a custom alpha above 0 means the Show animates EVE); `Combat: lifted Show key not restored yet, retrying: ...` until a lifted key comes back; a hit adds `reaction library|gate|tag ..., attack from the front|back|left|right (relative deg)` (or why it failed) and 0.3 s later `Combat: hit seq variant: head moved N cm; custom alpha A; additive alpha B; speed S cm/s` with `reaction kept` once a variant animates EVE, `inconclusive, EVE was already animating|moving` for a try that is not counted, or `no variant animated EVE, reactions off` after twelve counted tries, at most 200 lines per Lua lifetime. `/stellaravatar status` shows `Evades: unlocked F first, D double, C cut; locked F first, D double, C cut; N evade commands changed by the lock.`: Stellar Blade's own evade starts since the adapter loaded, as her command tree records them (a Show Lua plays, such as the perfect dodge or the lock's sword draw, is not one), split by whether the adapter applied Minecraft's lock. First counts every Evade-command row of her stance, so an air dodge, a fast getup or an Evade-command attack (flash-behind, move-back) adds one too; double counts her combo children (the double dodge); cut counts first dodges whose combo window (0.8 s for the sword evades) closed more than 0.2 s before its end, as when a skill outside her tree starts. N is how many lock-gated evade commands the latest locked frame changed (4 with the sword stance: both lock-on evades opened, both plain ones closed). For a double tap: one first and one double means the double dodge started; one first and one cut, that its window closed before the second press could take it; one first with neither, that the second press was not taken while the window was open; two firsts, that her tree went back to its root and the first row was taken again; a double counted without the expected animation points at the Show. The line is in the session's stop summary in Minecraft's log too. It ends with `Gauges: Beta current/max, Burst current/max` (`(locked)` before Stellar Blade unlocks Burst) or `Gauges not read.` when the adapter cannot reach the player actor, then `Stats: max HP H (health xA), attack P (strikes xB), critical C% xD, N shield blocks; hits taken xM melee, xR ranged.` with the latest stats Stellar Blade gave and what Minecraft makes of them (the health and strike multipliers, the share of a hit's damage EVE takes), or `Stats not read.` before the adapter could read them. Lua logs the lock-on as `Lock: proxy ..., tribe T for EVE tribe P, controller none`, `Lock: engaged: ...`, once a second `Lock: held, N relocks: ...` and `Lock: lost N: ...` when Stellar Blade drops it, each with `IsLockOn`, the AnimBP's `IsLockOn`, EVE's facing and the camera's angle off the target, the distance, her forward and sideways speed, the off-screen timer, movement mode and the flags Stellar Blade checks, the camera dolly (`dolly 0..1`, where 1 frames her from her hips) and `battle B sword State@Socket`, plus `Lock: sword N/5: drawing|instant draw (sword was State@Socket)` for each draw (or the error that stopped it) and `Lock: sword: sheathing ...`, `already sheathed ...` or `left to Stellar Blade ...` on release (after an attack while locked, the held lines keep `battle true` until the release, where `left to Stellar Blade` comes before Stellar Blade's own sheathe about 3 s later), then `Lock: released (reason): ...` (at most 300 lines; `held` and `lost` lines stop after 200). Combat (see the README) adds a line to `/stellaravatar status`: strikes landed, then perfect parries, parries, perfect dodges, dodges, guarded hits and hits taken. A strike counts once Minecraft accepted its damage; a defense counts once per attack (repeats within half a second keep the first verdict) for damage to the bridged player from an attacker or projectile while the link is live, in survival, and a hit only once it lands. Lua logs once per Lua lifetime the functions and properties named like combat (damage, hit, evade, parry, guard, actor state, time dilation and the like) on EVE's class chain and her components' classes, as `EVE combat: function Class.Name` or `EVE combat: property Class.Name` lines in `UE4SS.log` (at most 300): the starting point for Stellar Blade's own hit reactions and defensive state.

Lua logs the mob walls as `Mobs: first wall spawned, blocks channel N` (N is the object type of EVE's capsule, 24 or ECC_GameTraceChannel11 in the pinned build), `Mobs: EVE passes through characters: walls open` and `Mobs: EVE meets characters: walls solid` when Stellar Blade's own pass-through (`bIgnoreCharacterCollision`, set during her evades) changes, `Mobs: released N (stale)` (or `link lost`, `error`, `retry`, `F11 return requested`), `Mobs: error (n/3): ...` (three errors stop new walls until the Lua mod reloads) and `Mobs: release failed, retrying: ...`; `STOP: Mob walls paused: ...` reports an unexpected failure. Minecraft logs `Mob walls not delivered to Stellar Blade: ...` when it cannot write `eve-mobs.txt`.

### Shader packs

Experimental, not yet validated in game (no Iris build has been installed yet). While an Iris shader pack is in use, EVE is drawn through Minecraft's cut-out and translucent entity programs and the additive eyes program, which the pack replaces with its own (see the [architecture](architecture.md)); the log says `EVE avatar: drawing through Minecraft's entity programs (shader pack in use).` and, once the pack is off, `EVE avatar: drawing with her own shaders.` The first draw with each program logs `EVE avatar: Minecraft program NAME (ID) reads INPUT=array|constant ...`; under a pack, Iris's program names and `iris_` inputs show that the pack replaced Minecraft's programs. Under a pack:

- the pack lights, fogs and post-processes her as an entity from her base color only: normal, surface (occlusion, roughness, metallic, specular), emissive, subsurface and hair maps and the reflected sky are not used;
- cut-out sections use the entity alpha test (alpha 0.1) instead of 0.5 (0.25 for hair), so hair edges look denser and the faint fringes of some hair cards and pins can show light;
- translucent overlays (eye light, tear line, eye shadow, teeth occlusion) blend through the translucent entity program as they blend in her own shaders; the sword edge's glow is its texture times its streamed tint and opacity, added like glowing eyes;
- the sword trail and the effect sprites are added through the eyes program (the trail with its glow baked into a gray texture); packs usually treat that as emissive;
- she casts no shadow in the pack's shadow pass, and packs that tessellate entities may not draw her;
- the hurt flash, combat, lock-on, HUD and camera are unchanged; the lock-on outline is Minecraft's glowing outline, which depends on the pack.

Fabulous graphics need nothing special: EVE draws into Minecraft's main framebuffer before its transparency composition, so translucent blocks in front of her composite correctly; the additive trail and sprites, which write no depth, can be covered by translucent blocks that stand behind them (between them and the background), while glass in front of her covers her and the trail as it should. `./gradlew avatarRenderCheck` with a last argument `entity` renders through Minecraft's own entity programs from the client jar (no pack), with the sword glow, to check this path offscreen.

## Performance measurements

Each layer reports its own rate. Compare runs only at the same Minecraft framebuffer size, because capture cost grows with captured pixels.

- **Stellar Blade:** while a link is entered, `UE4SS.log` receives one `Cadence:` line per ten native seconds: native FPS, whether capture is live, completed generations and native frames from submission to observed completion. Idle lines give the same session's uncaptured rate.
- **Native adapter:** the private `eve-raw-timing.txt` keeps per-plane rows for the first 12 generations and rewrites whole-run summaries at most once per second. `COST 0`/`COST 2` are color/depth conversion times, `GAP` is the time between a generation's two plane arrivals: the second engine readback plus any image-queue wait, and `PERIOD` is the interval between publications. Values are count, mean and maximum microseconds.
- **Minecraft:** `/stellarview status`, also logged when a view stops, gives displayed frames, frames that arrived from the worker, frames replaced before the next Minecraft render or rejected as stale or mismatched, Minecraft world renders per second while the view is requested, and worker copy and texture-upload times. Upload time is the render-thread call cost, not GPU completion.

GPU contention between the games is outside these counters; sample it externally, for example with `nvidia-smi pmon`.

## Reverse terrain and texture probes

The reverse renderer displays Minecraft faces inside Stellar Blade and leaves its surroundings visible. It supports air and full cubes of stone, dirt, grass without snow, white wool and bedrock within the 9 × 6 × 9 blocks around the anchor. Unsupported blocks stop visual publication. Textures come from the active Minecraft resource manager; no game assets are packaged here. It does not import mobs, distant chunks, arbitrary models or fluids.

After `/stellar start`, F5 and F8, toggle it with `sbmc visuals`. Disable it before capturing EVE into Minecraft. Visual revisions are independent of collision revisions; changing wool to stone can update appearance without changing collision. F11 removes owned visuals and performs the guarded return.

The standalone `sbmc texture` probe requires `minecraft-stone.png` in the private bridge directory, extracted from `assets/minecraft/textures/block/stone.png` in the user's own Minecraft 1.21.1 client JAR. It creates one temporary non-colliding cube ahead of EVE. Its opaque display material does not establish Minecraft lighting compatibility.
